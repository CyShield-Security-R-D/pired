# ADR draft (3.1) — Findings are settled by someone other than their finder, on quotes checked against the code

**Status:** draft · 2026-09-26 · Phase 3 item 3.1 of `docs/delegation-v6-plan.md` · design in
`docs/delegation-v6-phase3-design.md` §3.1 · amends ADR-007 (the bars), ADR-028 (verifier rules)
and ADR-029 (the caps) · built on branch `p3a/verifier`; nothing merges to `main` until the Phase 4
trial passes

### Context

- **The precision problem is in both arms.** Absolute precision is 4–10% for the baseline and the
  candidate alike (`research/12-delegation-trial-2026-09.md`). The key labels only known CVE
  locations, so that figure is a comparison between arms, not a verdict on either, until claims are
  adjudicated.
- **Delegation added claims, mostly from workers.** On Luna v5c the candidate made 115 claims to the
  baseline's 58 for 7 true positives to 6, and 51 of those 115 claims were recorded by workers
  (research/12). Grid-pooled precision fell from 0.103 to 0.061 and pass-mean tokens rose 62%
  (ADR-026, *Outcome*). On GLM (commandcode) and DeepSeek there was no inflation.
- **Self-confirmations were not grounded.** On the 2026-09-24 grid, baseline parents confirmed and
  refuted by asserting `live=true` on scopes with no target: 17 of 18 of their verification records
  (ADR-028). A verifier that honestly said
  `live=false` could not refute anything, and 35 of 37 candidate findings ended `suspected`.
- **A verifier could rewrite what it checked.** Two verifiers re-recorded findings with new
  descriptions, and the replayed ledger fell from 4 true positives to 2 (ADR-028).
- **The literature research/12 cites** says verifiers work when they check external facts, not
  when they re-read the reasoning: grounding the check in path feasibility raised precision from
  0.28 to 0.93 at a cost of 25% of true positives (2601.18844); removing RepoAudit's validator halved
  its precision; prompted self-critique barely helps (Kamoi et al.); judges favour their own
  outputs (2404.13076), so a verifier should start from a fresh context and, where possible, be a
  different model.
- **The cost of delegation is the parent's turns.** 78% of the Luna candidate's extra parent
  tokens came from more turns at the baseline's growth rate; handoffs carried in context were 4%
  (research/12). A verifier's cost to the lead is the turns spent launching it and reading its
  return.
- **The official scorer ignores status.** A refutation cannot move official true positives, so a
  verifier's rejections, right or wrong, are invisible unless they are counted separately.
- **ADR-028 rejected `by` and grounding cited lines.** Row provenance was rejected because nothing
  depended on it; grounding the finding's own cited line was rejected because it cost true
  positives. The Phase 3 groundwork added `by`, since ADR-030 and this change depend on it.

### Decision

- **Provenance.** Every row carries `by` (groundwork), and every verification its own `by`,
  stamped from where the call ran and never from the caller. A finding that another author changed
  through a merge lists that author in `contributors` (additive); the creator and the contributors
  are its *finders*. A confirmation by none of them is *independently verified*; one by any of them
  is *verified by its finder*. The scribe counts as the lead, whose buffer it transcribes.
- **The grounded contract.** The model sends `path`, `guards` and `basis` as string lists of
  `"file:line: verbatim text"`; `path` and `guards` are stored as `{at, quote}`, a quote that is
  only a comment marked `commentOnly`. The record tool reads each cited file through the `Fs` port
  under the **engagement root** — the lead's directory, published to workers beside the scope, so a
  worker launched in a subdirectory names files the way the lead does — trying the path, the path
  from the writer's cwd, then the basename. Two files of one basename are reported as ambiguous, and
  a basename search cut short by its bound says so rather than trust one match. Only on an
  engagement with a live target is the workspace's `evidence/` read too: on a review of source a
  verifier's own `record kind=evidence` excerpt grounded a sink hop in review. `at` is stored
  relative to the root. `judge()` stays pure; `groundQuotes` takes a reader.
- **The grounding rule is frequency, not length.** A quote grounds when, after whitespace is
  normalised, it lies within **3 lines** of the cited line **and occurs at most 3 times in its
  file**, with a floor of **6 non-space characters**. A cited range is at most the first line plus
  two windows (7 lines). What makes a quote guessable is how common it is in that file: the first
  draft's 12-character minimum passed `return nil, err`, which a Go handler repeats after most error-returning
  calls, and failed `if not user:`, a guard a file has once. An unbounded range read the whole file
  as one window, so the decoy's guarded twin thirty lines away grounded. All the constants were
  fixed before any v6 run and are never tuned on corpus runs.
- **The bars** (`core/challenge.ts` `judge`):
  - any ungrounded quote: the verdict is `unresolved`, the status does not move (ADR-007), and the
    reply prints the text actually at the cited line, so a model that paraphrased or miscounted can
    fix it in one turn;
  - confirm on a source-backed engagement (`isSourceOnly`): `control` plus a `path` of at least two
    grounded hops, the first the source and the last the sink;
  - confirm on a live target: unchanged — control, and entitlement for the access-control classes
    (ADR-009);
  - refute on a source-backed engagement: `basis` plus at least one grounded quote — the guard, or
    the line that makes it not a finding, which is the adjudication rubric's `guarded` label;
  - refute on a live target: unchanged — a cited basis and a reachable target.
- **The tier** is derived, never taken. `executed` only on an engagement with a live target, and
  only when the check was live against it or cites a saved exchange with it — a request and its
  status line, both. A review of source is always `static`: `live=true` there describes nothing that
  ran (ADR-028), and evidence that shows neither a request nor a response (notes, a tool's summary)
  never counts. A `cat -n` excerpt no longer reads as a response. It is a label, not a gate.
- **A downgrade is a count.** An `unresolved` verification that missed its bar stores `attempted`
  (the verdict asked for) and `ungrounded` (how many quotes missed), so the quote-downgrade rate is
  computed from fields, not from the wording of notes.
- **Who may set which status** (`core/child.ts`, refusing with a reason, ADR-028):
  - Once a verification has settled a finding (`isEarned`), its finder may not change its status or
    its claim (title, description, target, severity) by id — refused in the gate and in the record
    tool, a second launch of the same worker name included; the write-up fields stay open, and a
    grounded look is still a look. In review, an investigator undid a verifier's confirmation by id.
    Any merge, the lead's included, into a row with an earned verification keeps its status and
    verification; a verdict is recorded by id, where it is judged against the one standing.
  - A worker whose role could not be read is held to the finder's rules as `worker`.
  - `pired-investigate` and `pired-escalate` create findings that land `suspected`;
    `status=confirmed` or `refuted` is refused with a reason naming the independent verifier, and so
    are the operator's dispositions. By id they change only findings they recorded; a create that
    merges into another author's finding is allowed, and the record tool keeps the stored status and
    verification on a worker's merge.
  - `pired-verify` may write `confirmed`, `refuted` or `suspected` with grounds, now including `path`
    and `guards`. A finding it creates lands `suspected` even when it carries `confirmed`: the record
    tool does not judge a worker's create.
  - Every worker is refused `kind=settle` (3.2 builds settle).
  - The lead may do anything; its confirm and refute are judged, and a self-confirmation is kept and
    labelled.
- **What the lead and the report show.** The unchallenged brief counts the waiting findings workers
  recorded and names up to six. `recall findings`, the `recall challenge` queue and `/challenge`
  carry `· by <author>` and, on a settled verdict, who judged it and the tier. The export shows
  Recorded by, Verified by (or Refuted by) and Tier; marks a confirmation "independently verified"
  or "verified by its finder"; keeps unverified worker findings in Suspected, marked as recorded by a
  worker; and prints a refutation with its quoted guard.
- **The verifier and its cost.** `pired-verify` stays the pi-subagents worker (ADR-027): a fresh
  context, `read` and `bash`, the tested gate.
  - A separate `maxVerifiers` cap: above 0, a verify launch is charged to it and not to `maxAgents`,
    refunded to it when it started nothing, and never falls back on `maxAgents` when spent. At 0 (the
    default) nothing changes. `hiddenParentTools` keeps `subagent` visible when it is above 0.
  - `maxAgents: 0, maxVerifiers: N` is the verify-only configuration, for the single agent, with its
    own delegation block. It is opt-in and measured as its own arm.
  - One late batch: the verify budget rises from 24 to 40 turns. This applies whenever
    `pired-verify` is launched, under plain `maxAgents` too, so it is a changed variable of the
    delegation arm as well as of the verify-only one, and the pre-registration names it — about five findings, at the
    roughly eight turns a finding a measured five-finding batch took (settling two in 16–18 turns,
    ADR-028) — and its handoff is at most 60 words of ids and verdict words. The delegation blocks
    say a verified row needs no re-reading.
  - An optional `verifier.model` in the scope registers `pired-verify` on another model.
    Operator-only; per-launch overrides are still refused.
- **Making rejected true positives measurable.** A refutation is a status, never a deletion.
  `eval/rescore.ts` partitions each run by claim tier — all, presented, suspected, confirmed and
  confirmed-independent, each matched from its own rows — by author, by the confirmed rows'
  verification tier, and by rows ever refuted in their JSONL history and who refuted them; and it
  counts verifications, downgrades and quote downgrades per run. `eval/adjudication.md` judges refuted rows with their
  status blinded.
- **Pinned before any model call.** A synthetic decoy fixture in `core/challenge.test.ts` — each
  sink beside a guarded twin, written for the test, not taken from any corpus application — pins
  `groundQuotes` and `judge`.

### Every text change the model can see (confound risk)

These move together with the rules, so a trial cannot attribute an effect to one of them.

1. **`record` description.** The finding status paragraph gains: "On a review of source the proof is
   quoted, not named: a confirmation carries 'path' (the source hop to the sink hop) and a
   refutation quotes the guard that makes it not a finding (in 'basis' or 'guards'), each as
   'file:line: verbatim text'. pired checks every quote against the file; one that is not there
   within 3 lines settles nothing, and the reply shows what the line does say."
2. **`record` schema.** `path` becomes string-or-list, described for both kinds (an endpoint's route;
   a verdict's source-to-sink hops). New `guards`. `basis` says a `file:line: text` entry is a quote
   and is checked.
3. **Worker missions.** `pired-verify`: the grounded quote, what it proves and what happens when it
   is not there; a created finding lands suspected and its verdict is a second write; 40 turns; a
   60-word handoff. `pired-investigate` and `pired-escalate`: their findings land suspected for a
   verifier or the lead, and the refusals they will meet.
4. **Delegation blocks.** The full block: a verify launch takes a batch of about five; verifier
   launches are counted apart when `maxVerifiers` is set; worker findings land suspected; a verified
   row needs no re-reading. The verify-only block is new.
5. **The unchallenged brief** (standing, per request) gains the count of waiting findings workers
   recorded.
6. **Tool output.** The refusals in `core/child.ts`, the ungrounded-quote reply, the worker-create
   note, and the `· by` labels on recall lines.
7. **Skills.** `ops-challenge-finding` §1a (what a grounded quote is and why) and `ops-delegation`
   §0c (why a finder does not settle its own findings; the batch shape; the cap).

### Consequences

- **Most current self-confirmations are demoted.** On a source-backed engagement a confirmation
  without a quoted path, or a refutation without a quoted guard, stays `suspected`. That is the
  intended bar and will read as fewer confirmed findings in the first runs.
- **The official number cannot move.** It grades every row; the claim tiers are where this shows.
- **Quote fidelity differs by model.** Some paraphrase or truncate, which gives more downgrades, so
  tiers are not comparable across models. The quote-downgrade rate is reported per model.
- **A same-family verifier favours the finder.** `verifier.model` exists for that; in the benchmark
  its provider's host must be in the sealed allowlist, and the adjudication judge must come from a
  third model family.
- **`by` is the agent, not the launch.** Two launches of `pired-investigate` share a name, so the
  second may edit the first's finding before it is verified, and the pair counts as one finder.
- **The verify budget changed for the delegation arm too.** 40 turns applies under plain
  `maxAgents`, so a comparison with the v5 candidate changes this variable as well.
- **Live-target refutations are unchanged.** A refutation on a live target still rests on a cited
  basis and a reachable target; a quote there is optional, and checked when given.

### Rejected

- **Grounding the finding's own cited line** (ADR-028's rejection stands). Only the verification's
  quotes are checked, a failed check only blocks promotion, and the finding's `target` is never
  touched.
- **A new status for "unverified".** `suspected` already means nobody has challenged it.
- **Stripping a worker's confirm instead of refusing it.** The model would read the write as a
  verdict recorded (ADR-028). The verifier's own create is the exception: it is not refused but
  lands suspected, because its linked create is how an overturned clearance reaches the report.
- **A native verifier loop** (ADR-027). The worker is already the tested gate.
- **A minimum quote length as the grounding rule** (the first draft's 12 characters). Length is not
  what makes a quote guessable; frequency in the file is. It passed common lines and failed rare
  guards.
- **Reading the workspace's evidence for a quote on a review of source.** A verifier could ground a
  hop in an excerpt it saved itself.
- **Counting any non-excerpt evidence as `executed`.** Notes and tool summaries showed nothing ran
  against a target; only a saved request and response does, and only where a target exists.
- **Tuning the window, the occurrence cap or the floor on runs.** Fixed from the shape of source lines; a
  tolerance fitted to what the benchmark's verifiers cited would measure the benchmark.
- **Requiring a quote on a live-target refutation.** Its grounds are the control case on the wire;
  `eval/field/reference.ts` refutes that way. It may quote a saved transcript instead, which also
  makes it `executed`.
- **Letting a spent `maxVerifiers` fall back on `maxAgents`.** The two caps would then be one, and
  verification would compete with work launches again.
- **A separate parameter name for the hops.** `path` is the ledger's name and the design's; a second
  name for either meaning is the one a model guesses wrong.

### Measurement

As designed (§3.1 *Measurement*), pre-registered in 4.2. Before any model call, the decoy fixture
pins `groundQuotes` and `judge`. In the trial, true positives at every rescore level and precision
(key-matched and adjudicated) are reported for each claim tier. It should move confirmed-tier
precision above suspected-tier precision, and presented precision up against the v5 candidate, with
rejected true positives within a pre-set share, the literature's ~25% as the ceiling. It is
falsified if the confirmed and suspected tiers do not separate, rejected true positives exceed the
share, or median parent requests rise by more than about 2 per verify launch. The quote-downgrade
rate and the adoption of the new fields are reported per model.
